The Mid-Context Dead Zone in KV Cache Compression

Compression methods partition long context by positional bias rather than utility. Every method tested recovers one end of the stream or the other, and none recovers the middle — yet an oracle told what to keep recovers all of it, at the same budget.

Abstract

KV cache compression is evaluated on aggregate accuracy at a budget, which hides where a method succeeds. Measuring recall against distance from the query, we find recency methods recover only the near field, attention-accumulation methods only the far field, and no method at any budget recovers the middle. Across 228 middle-band trials spanning four heuristics, three learned policies, four budgets and five seeds: zero successes, while an uncompressed cache answers 100% of the same queries.

We give a mechanism — survival under repeated compression decays geometrically with distance, making recency emergent rather than chosen — and report three failed attempts to train past it.

An oracle retention control, identical in budget and eviction geometry but told which tokens carry the answers, scores 40/40 in every band. The dead zone is therefore not a capacity limit: a 2048-token cache holding the right tokens answers everything. The failure is observability — nothing available at write time identifies which tokens will matter.

recent and h2o are exact complements — each strong in one band, exactly zero in the other, neither reaching the middle. H2O's accumulated-attention sum favours tokens visible to the most query rows, which are the oldest, so it inverts recency rather than improving on it. Neither bias tracks whether a token is useful.

Why: recency is emergent, not chosen

Compression runs every chunk, so the cache never exceeds B + chunk and each round discards only chunk of B + chunk tokens. A fact at distance d must survive d/chunk rounds:

P(survive) = ( B / (B + chunk) ) d / chunk

Any policy that does not actively protect distant tokens degenerates into recency for free. Compression is less a ranking problem than a rescue problem — the policy must counteract an exponential already running. This is why random admission and a recency window are empirically indistinguishable.

The first drowns its own signal: long-dependency positions are ~1.5% of a stream and carry only 14.9% of KL mass, so averaging hands 85% of the gradient to local prediction. The oracle: it is observability, not geometry

Three rewards landing on recency admits two explanations. Either the dead zone is intrinsic to fixed-budget streaming compression, or the information is retainable and nothing at write time identifies what to retain. One control separates them.

Oracle retention uses the identical budget, chunking, sink prefix, FIFO eviction and evaluation harness. One thing changes: the policy is told which spans carry the answers, scores them astronomically, and ranks everything else by recency.

The oracle matches the uncompressed cache exactly, at a quarter of the context, on every seed. Nothing about the budget, chunking, FIFO eviction or geometric survival prevents mid-context recall. The dead zone is not intrinsic.

What this makes of the training failures

They were not optimization failures. They were policies asked to predict, from write-time features alone, something the write-time input does not determine. A token's utility is fixed by a query that has not arrived and is not inferable from the token, its hidden state, its key norm, or its local attention.

The policies converged to recency because recency is optimal under that information constraint — when nothing distinguishes which distant token will matter, keeping the nearest maximises expected utility. The geometric decay of §4 is not a wall; it is the default any uninformed policy falls toward.

Coverage is unforgiving — missing a quarter of the answer tokens collapses middle-band recall from 7/7 to 1/7. An answer spans a few tokens, and retaining most of a six-digit code is worth nothing.

Precision is free until the budget binds. The flagged set is about fp · L, so at L=8192 and B=2048 the crowding threshold is fp ≈ 0.25. Beyond it, recall follows a second simple law:

recall = min( 1 , B / |flagged| )

Accurate to within 0.05 across the range. The specification is two-sided: a proxy must find essentially every token of an answer span, and keep its total flagged set within the budget. It may be indiscriminate about which extras it keeps, never about how many.

The consequence for method design is sharp. Reward shaping, credit assignment and optimizer stability at the admission decision are the wrong lever — those were the three things we tried. What is needed is signal write-time features lack: query-aware admission, deferred or revisable decisions, or a learned proxy for future queryability if one exists.

What would settle it

  1. Replicate on a second model and a real benchmark. Highest value, cheap, and the paper does not survive a failure to replicate.
  2. Query-aware admission. The oracle shows the capacity exists at B=2048; the question is how much of that gap a policy closes when it may consult the query at write time, converting an unanswerable prediction problem into a retrieval one.
  3. Partial-oracle ablation. Degrade the oracle — noisy spans, partial coverage, delayed knowledge — to measure how much signal is actually required, bounding how good a learned proxy would need to be.
  4. Finer distance resolution, to locate the boundary rather than assume a tercile.

Limitations

An earlier version of this draft argued the dead zone was probably intrinsic, on the strength of the three training failures. The oracle refutes that, and the error is recorded because the reasoning is a natural one to repeat: three failures under related objectives are weak evidence about a hypothesis space, while one control is decisive.

One model (Qwen3-1.7B) and one task family. Synthetic verbatim needle retrieval is the easiest possible case for a cache and the least like real long-context work; exact substring match scores a paraphrase as a miss. H2O and SnapKV are simplified reimplementations scored at token granularity, so absolute numbers are not comparable to published ones. "Middle" spans d≈2731–5461 — coarse. The intrinsic-limitation argument rests on three training runs with no hyperparameter search; a better-designed policy may yet succeed — indeed the oracle proves one can, though it requires knowing the answers in advance and so establishes capacity rather than a method. All results assume FIFO eviction with a fixed per-layer budget and a decision shared across layers, the last forced by a single global attention mask.

What is new here is the measurement, not the method. Write-time admission with a frozen backbone, RL for cache eviction, and Lagrangian KV allocation all exist in prior work; we are not aware of prior work reporting recall by distance-band.
