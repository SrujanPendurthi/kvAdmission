The Mid-Context Dead Zone in KV Cache Compression

Compression methods partition long context by positional bias rather than utility. Every method tested recovers one end of the stream or the other, and none recovers the middle — yet an oracle told what to keep recovers nearly all of it, at a quarter of the budget. It holds on synthetic needles and on real question answering alike.

Abstract

KV cache compression is evaluated on aggregate accuracy at a budget, which hides where a method succeeds. Measuring recall against distance from the query, we find recency methods recover only the near field, attention-accumulation methods only the far field, and no method at any budget recovers the middle. Across 284 middle-band trials on synthetic needles — two models, five heuristics, three learned policies, four budgets, five seeds — zero successes, while an uncompressed cache answers 100% of the same queries. On real planted-evidence QA the figure is 1/64 against an uncompressed 6/8.

We give a mechanism. Admission at rate p followed by FIFO eviction to budget B leaves a contiguous window over the most recent B/p positions, so recall is a step function with a hard horizon — past it, retention is exactly zero rather than merely unlikely. Recency is therefore emergent, not chosen. Three attempts to train past it fail, as does a policy allowed to read the pending queries at write time.

An oracle retention control, identical in budget and eviction geometry but told which tokens carry the answers, scores 40/40 in every band on Qwen3-1.7B. The dead zone is therefore not a capacity limit: a 2048-token cache holding the right tokens answers everything. The failure is observability — nothing available at write time identifies which tokens will matter. On SmolLM2-1.7B the same oracle recovers only 12/24, so how fully that capacity can be exploited is model-dependent, which we did not anticipate and cannot yet explain.

Finally we replace the synthetic task with planted-evidence QA over real SQuAD passages — human-written questions, natural-language answers, scored against a measured closed-book floor of 0/24. Every finding survives.

recent and h2o are exact complements — each strong in one band, exactly zero in the other, neither reaching the middle. H2O's accumulated-attention sum favours tokens visible to the most query rows, which are the oldest, so it inverts recency rather than improving on it. Neither bias tracks whether a token is useful.

And on real question answering

Synthetic verbatim recall was this work's weakest point: the easiest possible case for a cache, scored by a metric that counts a paraphrase as a miss. We replaced the task while keeping the distance axis everything depends on — distractor SQuAD paragraphs as the haystack, a real SQuAD paragraph as the planted evidence, the human-written question as the query, and a natural-language answer scored by containment with token F1 alongside.

Two controls that make the numbers readable

Closed-book floor: 0/24. Given the prompt and question but no haystack at all, the model answers none of them. Containment scoring is generous to short common-word golds, so without this control a lucky guess would be indistinguishable from a retrieval.

Distractor isolation. SQuAD groups paragraphs by article, so a same-article distractor can restate the answer. Distractors come only from articles no gold paragraph belongs to — otherwise a hit proves nothing about whether the planted evidence survived.

The oracle. 18/24 against the uncompressed 19/24, matching band for band, at a quarter of the context — while the best heuristic at the same budget manages 7/24. The gap between the oracle and the heuristics is far larger than the gap between the oracle and no compression at all.

One caveat is specific to this task: the far band has a difficulty ceiling, since full itself scores only 4/7 there. Far-band zeros are much weaker evidence here than on needles, where the ceiling was 1.00. The middle band carries the claim.

Correction to an earlier draft

This draft previously modelled survival as (B/(B+chunk))d/chunk — a token surviving d/chunk independent rounds. That is the law for random eviction. FIFO does not thin the cache uniformly; it removes the oldest deterministically, so the rounds are not independent. The geometric law predicts 0.38 survival at d=2048 and 0.16 in the middle band, against an observed 0/48 for every d ≥ 2048.

Its apparent fit came from tercile bands that straddled the horizon: band-level averaging hid a step. Coarse banding concealing a sharp transition is a general hazard in this measurement, and it caught us.

The correction sharpens the claim rather than weakening it. Distant tokens are not merely unlikely to survive — past the horizon they survive with probability exactly zero, so any policy that does not explicitly protect a token beyond B/p loses it with certainty. The middle band lies entirely outside the horizon at every budget tested. Compression is not a ranking problem but a rescue problem.

The first drowns its own signal: long-dependency positions are ~1.5% of a stream and carry only 14.9% of KL mass, so averaging hands 85% of the gradient to local prediction. The second, weighting positions by their own divergence, destabilised and collapsed to admitting nothing. The third fixed both — stable training, best checkpoint better on the training objective than the first (kl_raw 0.103 vs 0.121) — and was still behaviourally identical to a recency window.

The oracle: it is observability, not geometry

Three rewards landing on recency admits two explanations. Either the dead zone is intrinsic to fixed-budget streaming compression, or the information is retainable and nothing at write time identifies what to retain. One control separates them.

Oracle retention uses the identical budget, chunking, sink prefix, FIFO eviction and evaluation harness. One thing changes: the policy is told which spans carry the answers, scores them astronomically, and ranks everything else by recency.

The oracle doubles recent's total and produces the first non-zero middle-band cell any policy has reached on this model — but recovers half the trials, not all. The pattern is specific: perfect where retention coincides with contiguous recency (near, 9/9), poor wherever it depends on the flag.

Leading hypothesis — untested

Contiguity. The oracle's cache is spliced: answer spans from position ~2000 sit adjacent to recency fill from ~7000, with a gap in the position indices. Qwen3 appears to read a spliced cache; SmolLM2 appears not to, and degrades in exactly the pattern that predicts. This is fitted to three seeds. The direct control — an oracle keeping a contiguous block of equal size at equal distance — has not been run.

What replicates on both models is that the budget is not the binding constraint: the oracle beats every heuristic at the same budget on both. What does not replicate is that the uncompressed ceiling is reachable. Whether a model can exploit a non-contiguous retained set is a variable no prior work in this line varies, and on this evidence it matters.

What this makes of the training failures

They were not optimization failures. They were policies asked to predict, from write-time features alone, something the write-time input does not determine. A token's utility is fixed by a query that has not arrived and is not inferable from the token, its hidden state, its key norm, or its local attention.

The policies converged to recency because recency is optimal under that information constraint — when nothing distinguishes which distant token will matter, keeping the nearest maximises expected utility. The geometric decay of §4 is not a wall; it is the default any uninformed policy falls toward.

How good must a proxy be?

Degrading the oracle measures what a realisable signal would need. Coverage is its recall over answer tokens; fp its false-positive rate over everything else.

Coverage is unforgiving — missing a quarter of the answer tokens collapses middle-band recall from 7/7 to 1/7. An answer spans a few tokens, and retaining most of a six-digit code is worth nothing.

Precision is free until the budget binds. The flagged set is about fp · L, so at L=8192 and B=2048 the crowding threshold is fp ≈ 0.25. Beyond it, recall follows a second simple law:

recall = min( 1 , B / |flagged| )

Fitted on synthetic needles — and it transfers to the real-QA task above, where the eight gold paragraphs occupy 1332 tokens of real prose rather than 48 synthetic digit tokens. At B=2048 the flagged set fits and the oracle tracks the ceiling. At B=512 it overflows, and the predicted survival fraction of 0.38 matches the observed 0.38 exactly. That is why the oracle collapses at the smaller budget: not a failure of the signal, but the budget refusing to hold what the signal correctly identified.

Accurate to within 0.05 across the range. The specification is two-sided: a proxy must find essentially every token of an answer span, and keep its total flagged set within the budget. It may be indiscriminate about which extras it keeps, never about how many.

The consequence for method design is sharp. Reward shaping, credit assignment and optimizer stability at the admission decision are the wrong lever — those were the three things we tried. What is needed is signal write-time features lack: query-aware admission, deferred or revisable decisions, or a learned proxy for future queryability if one exists.

Knowing the question is not enough

The proxy specification asks for a signal that identifies answer tokens at write time. The obvious candidate is the query itself. We tested a policy scoring each token by maximum cosine similarity to a pooled embedding of the pending queries — already a semi-oracle, since a streaming policy would not hold the queries yet, so this is an upper bound on what write-time query access buys.

Zero in the middle band at both budgets, and it forfeits the near band that recency gets for free, inverting into a far-band bias like h2o.

The failure is informative because of where it sits. Query-aware admission has strictly more information than the heuristics and strictly less than the oracle, and it lands on the heuristic side of the gap. What the oracle holds is not "what is being asked" but "which tokens answer it," and embedding similarity does not recover the second from the first. An earlier version of this page recommended query-aware admission first. The framing survives; the cheap instantiation of it does not.

What would settle it

All four items previously listed here have been run: the second-model replication, the finer distance sweep (which corrected the mechanism), query-aware admission (which failed), and the real-QA benchmark (where everything replicated). What remains:

  1. Real QA on the second model. The benchmark ran on Qwen3 only, and the oracle's SmolLM2 shortfall is exactly the kind of result that might or might not survive a change of task. Now the cheapest way to be wrong.
  2. The contiguity control. An oracle keeping a contiguous block versus scattered spans — same size, same distance. Decides whether that model-dependence is about the retained set's shape.
  3. Vary the admit rate. One run at p=0.5 tests the horizon law's only untested prediction.
  4. Retrieval over evicted tokens, rather than write-time query scoring. The cheap version of query-awareness is ruled out; the direction is not.

Limitations

An earlier version of this page argued the dead zone was probably intrinsic, on the strength of the three training failures. The oracle refutes that. The error is recorded because the reasoning is a natural one to repeat: three failures under related objectives are weak evidence about a hypothesis space, while a control changing one variable is worth more than all three. The model-dependence above is the same lesson applied to the control itself, which we had also treated as settled after one model. A third correction — the geometric survival law — is recorded in its own section.

Two models, one size class (Qwen3-1.7B, SmolLM2-1.7B-Instruct); the real-QA benchmark ran on Qwen3 only. Two task families, both short-answer: synthetic needles and planted-evidence SQuAD QA. The second fixes the corpus, query and metric, but extractive short-answer QA is still far easier than multi-hop reasoning, summarisation or agent traces, and neither task is a published benchmark run end to end. On real QA the far band has a difficulty ceiling — full scores 4/7 — so far-band zeros there are weak evidence, and n is small: 24 trials per cell, 8 per band, giving a 7.2% upper bound rather than 1%. The oracle ceiling does not replicate across the two models and we report a hypothesis we have not tested. H2O and SnapKV are simplified reimplementations scored at token granularity, so absolute numbers are not comparable to published ones. Three training runs, no hyperparameter search. The oracle requires knowing the answers in advance, so it establishes capacity rather than a method. Query-awareness is tested through one similarity function. All results assume FIFO eviction with a fixed per-layer budget and a decision shared across layers, the last forced by a single global attention mask. The horizon law is fitted on two admit rates, 1.000 and 0.941, which are close together.

What is new here is the measurement, not the method. Write-time admission with a frozen backbone, RL for cache eviction, and Lagrangian KV allocation all exist in prior work; we are not aware of prior work reporting recall by distance-band.
