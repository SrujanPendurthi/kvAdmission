\title{What a KV Cache Retention Signal Must Achieve:\\
       A Specification and a Test That Rejects Candidates for Nothing}
\author{}
\date{}

\begin{abstract}
KV cache compression methods proliferate, each proposing a rule for scoring
which tokens to keep, each evaluated on aggregate accuracy at a compression
ratio. What none of them states is the \emph{target}: what would a retention
signal actually have to achieve to work?

We answer with an oracle. Under a fixed budget, chunked prefill and FIFO
eviction, a policy \emph{told which tokens carry the answers} recovers
$40/40$ at 1.7B and $24/24$ at 8B---matching an uncompressed cache on a
quarter of the context---while the best heuristic at the same budget reaches
$7/24$ and no heuristic reaches the middle of the context at all. The capacity
is already there; what is missing is a signal.

Degrading that oracle yields a two-sided specification. \textbf{Coverage is
unforgiving}: retaining 75\% of answer tokens collapses mid-context recall
from $7/7$ to $1/7$. \textbf{Precision is free until the budget binds}, after
which recall follows $\min(1, B/|\text{flagged}|)$---a law that transfers
unchanged to a task it was not fitted on, predicting $0.38$ and observing
$0.38$.

The specification is then usable as a \textbf{screening test}: a candidate can
be rejected by measuring what fraction of answer spans falls inside its top
$B$, with no policy implemented and no GPU rented. We validate this once,
rejecting a candidate for \$0 with five pre-registered predictions all
confirmed, then apply it to \textbf{nine candidate signals across five
independent framings}. None passes. The candidate given 128 tokens of a
token's \emph{future} scores below chance.
\end{abstract}

\section{The missing target}

A long-context model writes every token into its KV cache; under a budget most
must go. The literature decides what to discard by ranking tokens---accumulated
attention \citep{zhang2023h2o}, observation-window voting
\citep{li2024snapkv}, recency plus sinks \citep{xiao2024streamingllm},
layer-wise allocation \citep{cai2024pyramidkv,feng2024adakv}---and reports
aggregate accuracy at a compression ratio.

That evaluation answers ``is this rule better than that one.'' It does not
answer ``how good would a rule have to be,'' and without that number a proposed
signal cannot be assessed except by building it and running it. Every candidate
costs an implementation and a GPU run, and a negative result teaches nothing
transferable. This paper supplies the missing target and makes it cheap to
check against.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}\itemsep2pt
\item An \textbf{oracle control} showing the capacity for mid-context retention
      already exists at two model scales---the failure is observability, not
      budget (\S\ref{sec:oracle}).
\item A \textbf{two-sided specification} for a realisable signal, both halves
      quantitative laws, one transferring to a task it was not fitted on
      (\S\ref{sec:spec}).
\item A \textbf{screening test} derived from it, validated with five
      pre-registered predictions on a candidate rejected for \$0
      (\S\ref{sec:test}).
\item \textbf{Nine candidate signals rejected} across five independent
      framings, with the near-miss showing precisely what the specification
      demands (\S\ref{sec:test}).
\item A \textbf{mechanism}---a hard retention horizon at $d = B/p$---explaining
      why position-based rules cannot clear the bar however they are tuned
      (\S\ref{sec:mech}).
\item A \textbf{controlled isolation of fragmentation} as a second constraint
      the specification does not capture (\S\ref{sec:frag}).
\end{enumerate}

\section{Setup}\label{sec:setup}

\textbf{Models.} Qwen3-1.7B, SmolLM2-1.7B-Instruct, Qwen3-8B; bf16, frozen. No
model weights are trained anywhere in this work.
\textbf{Streams.} \emph{Synthetic:} 8192 tokens of prose with eight facts
\texttt{The secret code for <topic> is <6 digits>} at jittered even anchors.
\emph{Real:} 8192 tokens of distractor SQuAD paragraphs with eight gold
paragraphs at the same anchors, queried by their human-written questions,
scored by containment and token F1 against a measured \textbf{closed-book floor
of $0/24$}. Distractors are drawn only from articles no gold paragraph belongs
to.
\textbf{Compression.} Chunked prefill at 128 tokens, compressed to budget $B$
per layer after each chunk, first four tokens pinned as sinks.

\paragraph{Baselines are scoring rules, not systems.}
\texttt{recent}, \texttt{h2o} and \texttt{snapkv} implement the
\emph{token-scoring rule} of StreamingLLM, H2O and SnapKV at token granularity
under a uniform per-layer budget---no per-head budgets, no layer allocation,
none of the systems engineering those papers contribute. Absolute numbers are
not comparable to published ones and nothing here benchmarks a published
system. What is under test is the scoring rule, because that is the component
the effect is a property of.

\paragraph{Harness.} At unbounded budget, logits are bit-identical to a stock
\texttt{DynamicCache} under the same chunking, verified on two backends.
Positions are passed explicitly, since compression makes cache length diverge
from stream position. 135 tests.

\section{The capacity already exists}\label{sec:oracle}

\textbf{Oracle retention} uses the identical budget, chunking, sink prefix,
FIFO eviction and evaluation as every baseline, through the same eviction path.
One thing changes: it is told which token spans carry the answers.

\begin{table}[h]\centering\small
\begin{tabular}{lcccc}
\toprule
policy, $B{=}2048$ & near & middle & far & total \\
\midrule
best heuristic       & 0.71 & \textbf{0.00} & 0.57 & $7/24$ \\
best learned policy  & 0.71 & \textbf{0.00} & 0.00 & --- \\
\textbf{oracle}      & \textbf{1.00} & \textbf{1.00} & \textbf{1.00} & $\mathbf{40/40}$ \\
\texttt{full} (uncompressed) & 1.00 & 1.00 & 1.00 & --- \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

$40/40$ at Qwen3-1.7B and $24/24$ at Qwen3-8B. A 2048-token cache holding the
\emph{right} 2048 tokens answers every query an uncompressed 8192-token cache
does. The gap this exposes is not between compressed and uncompressed but
between the oracle and every heuristic \emph{at the same budget}. What
separates them is information, not capacity.

\paragraph{Motivating context.} Across 340 middle-band trials on synthetic
needles---three models spanning a $4.7\times$ size range, five scoring rules,
three learned policies, four budgets, five seeds---we observe \textbf{zero
successes} (95\% upper bound 0.9\%). On real planted-evidence QA the figure is
$1/64$ against an uncompressed $6/8$. That compressed caches under-retain the
middle is consistent with prior reports (\S\ref{sec:related}); what makes it
the right motivation is that the oracle removes it entirely at the same budget.

\section{The specification}\label{sec:spec}

\subsection{Coverage is unforgiving}

\begin{table}[h]\centering\small
\begin{tabular}{lcc}
\toprule
coverage over answer tokens & overall & middle band \\
\midrule
1.00 & $24/24$ & $\mathbf{7/7}$ \\
0.75 & $12/24$ & $\mathbf{1/7}$ \\
0.50 & $8/24$  & $0/7$ \\
0.25 & $6/24$  & $0/7$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

Missing a quarter of the answer tokens collapses the middle band from $7/7$ to
$1/7$. An answer spans few tokens, and retaining most of a six-digit code is
worth nothing: the digits are mutually unpredictable, so every one must
survive. \textbf{A signal that is usually right about which tokens matter is
not good enough.}

\subsection{Precision is free until the budget binds}

Below the point where the flagged set exceeds $B$, false positives cost
nothing. Above it, recall follows
\begin{equation}
  \text{recall} \;=\; \min\!\left(1,\ \frac{B}{|\text{flagged}|}\right)
  \label{eq:crowding}
\end{equation}
accurate to $0.05$ across $\text{fp}\in[0.05,0.5]$. \textbf{It transfers to a
task it was not fitted on}: on real SQuAD QA the eight gold paragraphs occupy
1332 tokens of natural prose rather than 48 synthetic digit tokens, and at
$B{=}512$ Eq.~\ref{eq:crowding} predicts $0.38$ against an observed $0.38$.

\subsection{Stated}
\begin{quote}
A retention signal must identify \textbf{essentially every token} of an answer
span, and keep its \textbf{total flagged set within the budget}. It may be
entirely indiscriminate about which \emph{extra} tokens it keeps, so long as it
does not keep too many.
\end{quote}

\section{The specification as a test}\label{sec:test}

The coverage half is measurable \emph{without implementing a policy}: compute
the signal, take its top $B$, and ask what fraction of answer spans falls
inside. Anything short of ${\approx}1.00$ spans-retained-whole predicts
end-to-end failure.

\paragraph{Validating it.} Surprisal---score each token by
$-\log p(\text{token}\mid\text{prefix})$, keeping what the context cannot
reconstruct---is a principled, free, non-positional candidate. The screen
rejected it locally for \$0: coverage $0.107$ on needles against a $0.250$
chance rate, $0.248$ on real QA, $0/16$ spans whole on either.
\textbf{Five predictions were then committed to version control before a GPU
was rented.} All five held: needle middle band $0/7$, needle near band $0/9$
against \texttt{recent}'s $10/14$, needle overall $0/24$, QA overall $2/24$
with middle $0/8$, and beating \texttt{recent} on neither. Every number landed
at or below its predicted bound.

\begin{table}[h]\centering\small
\begin{tabular}{llcccc}
\toprule
signal & framing & needle cov. & QA cov. & spans whole \\
\midrule
learned admission $\times3$ & learned features & --- & --- & $0/12$ mid \\
query-aware retention & query similarity & --- & --- & $0/12$ mid \\
\texttt{surprisal} & information content & 0.107 & 0.240 & $0/16$ \\
\texttt{norm\_attention} & attention received & 0.230 & 0.290 & $\le 2/16$ \\
\texttt{key\_norm} & activation magnitude & 0.384 & 0.244 & $0/16$ \\
\texttt{rarity} & lexical statistics & 0.102 & 0.265 & $0/16$ \\
\texttt{answer\_shape} & answer form & \textbf{0.541} & 0.271 & $0/16$ \\
\texttt{lookahead} & 128 tokens of future & 0.163 & 0.228 & $0/16$ \\
\emph{random control} & --- & 0.248 & 0.249 & $0/16$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\caption{Nine candidates, none passing. Chance is $0.250$.}
\end{table}

Three entries carry the argument.

\textbf{\texttt{norm\_attention} fails, and it was the best available repair.}
Accumulated attention leans toward the start of the stream because the sum runs
over a variable number of query rows and early tokens are visible to more of
them; dividing by that count is the obvious fix. It lands \emph{below chance}
on needles. Removing the positional bias does not reveal a utility signal
underneath---it removes the bias producing H2O's only wins.

\textbf{\texttt{answer\_shape} is the strongest signal found and still fails.}
At $2.2\times$ chance on needles it does exactly what it was built to do: it
flags digits, and the answers are digits. It is a prior written with knowledge
of the answer \emph{format}. It retains $0/16$ spans whole, because half a
six-digit code is worth nothing. Knowing the form of the answer is not enough.

\textbf{\texttt{lookahead} fails below chance, and it was the hardest test.}
Every other candidate must infer a token's future from its past. This one is
\emph{given} 128 tokens of that future---the attention a token receives from
the following chunk, implementable by delaying compression a single chunk. It
scores $0.163$ and $0.228$, both below chance. A chunk's attention to its
predecessor is dominated by local syntactic continuation, so short-range
lookahead proxies very local recency, which is if anything anti-correlated with
being queried thousands of tokens later.

\begin{quote}
The information determining a token's retention value is not in its past, and
not in its immediate future either. It is in a query that has not arrived.
\end{quote}

\section{Why position-based rules cannot clear the bar}\label{sec:mech}

\input{figure}

Under admission at rate $p$ followed by FIFO eviction to budget $B$, occupancy
saturates and the cache becomes a contiguous window over the most recent $B/p$
raw positions:
\begin{equation}
  P(\text{token resident at distance } d) =
  \begin{cases} p & d < B/p \\ 0 & \text{otherwise.}\end{cases}
  \label{eq:horizon}
\end{equation}
\texttt{recent} ($p{=}1$) is predicted exactly: $16/16$ inside $d{=}B$,
$0/48$ outside. Tested at $p{=}0.5$, where the horizon should double:
$2/32$ inside 4096, $0/32$ beyond.

Beyond the horizon, survival is \emph{exactly} zero, not merely unlikely. Any
rule that does not actively protect a token past $B/p$ loses it with certainty,
so recency is not a design choice but the attractor every uninformed rule
decays onto. \texttt{h2o} has the mirror-image horizon
(Fig.~\ref{fig:horizon}). \textbf{The two families are exact complements}: at
$B{=}2048$, \texttt{recent} scores $10/14$ near and $\mathbf{0/14}$ far;
\texttt{h2o} scores $\mathbf{0/14}$ near and $8/14$ far. Both are functions of
position; neither is a function of utility.

\section{A constraint the specification misses}\label{sec:frag}

The specification concerns \emph{which} tokens are retained, not their
arrangement---and arrangement matters. Holding the stream, needles, distances,
flagged token count (1600) and recency fill constant, and varying only the
number of contiguous runs the retained evidence occupies:

\begin{table}[h]\centering\small
\begin{tabular}{lccc}
\toprule
runs & window & SmolLM2-1.7B & Qwen3-1.7B \\
\midrule
\textbf{8} & 200  & \textbf{0.54} [0.35, 0.72] & 0.96 \\
\textbf{4} & 400  & \textbf{0.92} [0.74, 0.98] & 1.00 \\
2 & 800  & 0.83 [0.64, 0.93] & 1.00 \\
1 & 1600 & 0.79 [0.60, 0.91] & 1.00 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

Fisher exact, 8 versus 4: $p = 0.0078$. The effect is confined to the dead
zone---SmolLM2's near band is perfect at every setting---and width does not
explain it: a separate sweep varied width at fixed count over a $13\times$
range and moved $0.50 \to 0.58$. That fragmentation harms compressed caches is
known \citep{yang2026ams}; what this adds is the controlled attribution to
fragment \emph{count}, and the consequence for \S\ref{sec:spec}: a signal can
satisfy the specification exactly and still fail by delivering the right tokens
in too many pieces.

\section{Limitations}

\textbf{The test has only ever said no.} One successful rejection validates it;
a signal that \emph{passes} and then succeeds would test the other direction,
and we have none. We attempted to produce a positive case with
\texttt{lookahead} and it failed the screen too, so this stands open. A screen
that rejects everything is consistent with being correct and with being
uselessly conservative.
\textbf{The oracle is an upper bound, not a method}---it requires knowing the
answers in advance. This paper specifies a target and supplies a test; it does
not supply a signal meeting the target.
\textbf{Two task families, both short-answer}, neither a published benchmark
run end to end; the real-QA benchmark discriminates only on Qwen3-1.7B.
\textbf{Three models, 1.7B--8B}, one architecture family plus one outsider.
\textbf{Scoring rules, not systems} (\S\ref{sec:setup}).
\textbf{One architectural family}: FIFO eviction, fixed per-layer budget,
decisions shared across layers---the last forced by a single global attention
mask.
\textbf{The coverage curve is measured on the synthetic task only}; its
transfer rests on Eq.~\ref{eq:crowding}'s transfer, which was verified, not on
a second coverage sweep.

\section{Related work}\label{sec:related}

\textbf{Position bias without compression.} \citet{liu2024lost} show a U-shaped
accuracy curve over the position of relevant information in \emph{uncompressed}
context. Our \texttt{full} baseline scores $1.00$ in every band on all three
models, so at these lengths the middle is answered perfectly without a budget:
the effect we motivate with is created by compression rather than inherited
from the model.

\textbf{Position-resolved evaluation of compression.} \citet{indexmem2026}
report needle-in-a-haystack heatmaps over context length and needle depth and
observe position-dependent failure of SnapKV and PyramidKV. Reporting recall by
position is therefore not new; what \S\ref{sec:mech} adds is the complementarity
and the horizon mechanism.

\textbf{Fragmentation.} \citet{yang2026ams} name \emph{Region Wipe-out}---global
top-$k$ triggers severe eviction of contiguous blocks---report that token-wise
selection shows a lost-in-the-middle retention pattern, and propose region-aware
segment quotas. \S\ref{sec:frag}'s phenomenon and its remedy are theirs, in a
decoding-time chain-of-thought setting; our contribution there is the controlled
attribution to fragment count.

\textbf{Non-uniform damage.} \citet{chen2025pitfalls} evaluate five methods on
Llama3-8B and Qwen2.5-14B under multi-instruction prompting, finding some
instructions degrade far faster than others. Same theme, different axis: their
unit is an instruction, ours a fact at a distance.

\textbf{Methods.} Write-time admission with a frozen backbone \citep{kvadmission2025};
RL for post-hoc eviction \citep{learningtoevict2026}; online Lagrangian KV
allocation \citep{triroute2026}.

\textbf{What is new} is not that compression hurts, nor where. It is the
\emph{target}---an oracle establishing the capacity exists, and two laws saying
what a signal must achieve to reach it---and the \emph{test} turning that target
into a rejection criterion costing nothing to apply.

\textbf{What we do not claim.} We did not run H2O, SnapKV or StreamingLLM as
published systems and do not report that they fail on their own benchmarks.
Whether the full systems inherit these effects is our prediction, not our
measurement.
